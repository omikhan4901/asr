How We Label

The guide for the Bangla ASR labeling work: references, error audit, word pairs, and settling disagreements.
CSE499B · Omi, Nabigah, NafisaVersion 1 · 9 October 2026Site: asr-labelling-kappa.vercel.app

1. Why this work matters

Our labels are the only part of the project that a computer cannot produce. They answer three questions the paper depends on:

  • Are the dataset's transcripts right? If a transcript is wrong, the model gets blamed for an error it did not make. Your corrected references tell us how often that happens.
  • Are the "spelling errors" really spelling? Our program guesses that about 40% of the model's errors are the same word written differently (ছিল / ছিলো). Your judgments tell us whether that guess is true. This decides the main claim of the paper (Gate 3).
  • Can other people trust our labels? Some items are labeled by two or three of us without talking. How often we agree (called κ, "kappa") goes in the paper as proof that the labels are reliable.
Three things matter more than speed 1. Consistency: all three of us follow exactly the same rules. A rule we apply differently makes our agreement look bad, even if each of us was "right".
2. Independence: never discuss an item before all three have labeled it. Disagreements are settled together at the end, on a separate screen.
3. Honesty: when you are not sure, say so (with a note or "Can't tell"). A guess that looks confident is worse than an honest "unclear".

Our labels are used only to measure results, never to train or tune a model. That is why they must reflect the audio, not what we think the model or the paper needs.

In total that is about 2,800 labels, roughly 19 hours each. Shared items are served first, so the agreement check can start early.

Order: References first, then the error audit, then word pairs. References come first because you should hear and fix the clips before you have seen what the recognizer wrote.

Sessions: work in blocks of 45–60 minutes with a break. Use headphones. Labeling quality drops quickly when you are tired; stop and come back.

4. The golden rules

Almost every hard case is answered by one of these. When unsure, come back to this page.

  1. Write what you hear, not what the context suggests. If the speaker clearly says ভাবে where ভাবি is meant, write ভাবে and add the note means ভাবি. Never "fix" a word because the sentence would make more sense.
  2. Fix words, never punctuation. Ignore । , ? ! quotes and the >> speaker marks, even when they are wrong. Scoring removes them anyway. If only punctuation is off, the clip is Correct as is.
  3. Do not change a valid spelling. ছিল / ছিলো, কি / কী, কোন / কোনো, হল / হলো: if the spoken word is written in any accepted spelling, leave it.
  4. Dialect word forms are written as spoken. Speaker says কইরা and the text has করে: change it to কইরা. Same for নিয়া, তুমার, যাইতাছি. If the speaker uses the standard form, keep it.
  5. An accent on one sound keeps the standard spelling. Speaker says ঢেকালো for ঠেকালো, or "bhuk" for ভুখ: write the standard spelling and add the note accent. The difference between rules 4 and 5: in a dialect form the word changes (করে → কইরা); in an accent one sound is said differently but it is the same written word.
  6. Do not guess. If you cannot make out a word after listening several times, keep the original word and add the note unclear. If you cannot make out the main speech at all, choose Can't use.
  7. Work alone on shared items. Do not ask each other about an item until it is in "Settle disagreements".

6. Task 2: Error audit

You see the clip, the transcript from the dataset, and a recognizer's output. Every place where they differ is highlighted. Select a difference (or let the site move through them) and press a key:

KeyLabelChoose it whenExample
1Recognizer wrongThe transcript matches the audio, the recognizer does not.
2Transcript wrongThe recognizer matches the audio, the transcript does not.transcript ভোগ, recognizer ভুখ, speaker says ভুখ
3Both wrongNeither matches what is said.
4Same word, spelling differsBoth write the spoken word correctly, in different accepted spellings, or split differently.ছিল / ছিলো, কি / কী, দু পাশে / দুপাশে
5Same word, dialect vs standardOne side writes the spoken dialect form, the other the standard form. Also an accent on one sound.করে / কইরা, তোমার / তুমার, ঠেকালো / ঢেকালো
6Can't tellThe audio is unclear at this spot.

How to decide, in order

  1. Listen to the spot. Judge against the audio, never against which version looks nicer or makes more sense.
  2. Is it the same word on both sides? If yes: two accepted spellings → 4; a dialect form or an accent → 5.
  3. If not the same word: whose word matches the audio? Transcript → 1; recognizer → 2; neither → 3.
  4. Still unsure after listening again → 6. Do not guess.
Common traps
  • A missing or extra word is not a spelling difference. If the recognizer left out a spoken word, that is 1; if the transcript has a word nobody says, that is 2.
  • আর / আরো, এখন / এখনো, করে / করা are different words (meaning or grammar changes), so they are 1, 2 or 3, not 4.
  • When the speaker clearly says the recognizer's word but context suggests the transcript's, the transcript is wrong: 2 (golden rule 1).

7. Task 3: Word pairs

You see a word from a transcript (left) and the word a recognizer wrote instead (right), with two example sentences. Decide what kind of difference it is; the key saves and moves on.

KeyLabelMeaningExamples
1Same word, different spellingSame word and meaning, written two accepted ways.ছিল / ছিলো, কি / কী, রং / রঙ, কোন / কোনো
2Dialect or colloquial formThe same word said the regional or spoken way, including an accent on one sound.নিয়ে / নিয়া, তোমার / তুমার, ঠেকালো / ঢেকালো
3Different wordThe meaning or grammar changes.আর / আরো, এখন / এখনো, করে / করা
4Can't tellThe examples do not make it clear.

Judge the two words, using the example sentences only for context. There is no audio in this task: you are not judging who was right, only what kind of difference it is.

9. Settling disagreements

When everyone has finished a shared item and the answers differ, it appears under Settle disagreements on the home screen. Do this together (in person or on a call), after your separate sessions.

  1. Open the task's button (it shows how many are open). Items come one by one.
  2. References show what each person changed. In the original transcript, words someone changed are highlighted. In each answer, removed words are struck through and new words are green, with a summary line such as "Changes: ভাবে → ভাবি · ভোগ → ভুখ". So you only listen to the spots that differ.
  3. Listen to those spots together and apply the golden rules. Press Use this text on the closest answer, edit the Agreed text if needed, choose the status and press Save agreed answer.
  4. Error audit shows every difference with each person's label; only the ones that differ need an agreed choice. Word pairs show everyone's label; pick the agreed one.
  5. If, after listening together, you still cannot agree, use the honest answer: the original word with unclear (references) or Can't tell (audit, pairs).

Your original answers are never changed: the agreed answer is stored separately. Agreement (κ) is computed from the original, independent answers, so it is important that those were made alone.
